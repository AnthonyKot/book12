# AGENT.md — instructions for the writing model (book 12)

Read this BEFORE drafting or revising any chapter. Precedence: CONTEXT.md (rules) →
this file (what the book is for) → TEMPLATE.md (chapter rhythm) → prose. Nothing here
overrides CONTEXT.md's voice rules or the quotes-or-nothing law.

## Who is reading

An adult with a passport they never had to earn. They took no test, signed no charter,
swore no oath — citizenship arrived with their birth certificate. They will stop
reading the moment the book feels like civics homework. They stay for two things:
watching states describe themselves to strangers (the comparison), and the slow
realization that they could not pass the test their own country sets (the hook).
Every chapter must deliver both.

## Priority stack (in order; higher wins on conflict)

1. **Never false.** Every claim checkable; every document claim quoted; every
   enrichment claim mainstream-verifiable. Being boring is a failure; being wrong
   is a betrayal.
2. **The reader must be IN the chapter** — see the entrance-exam rule below.
3. **Comparison is the skeleton.** The ladder, the originals, the tells.
4. **The missing piece is evidence** — but graded against the corpus baseline
   (CONTEXT.md): an omission shared by most guides is genre norm, not national
   avoidance; a topic only one or two guides carry is a signature.

## The entrance-exam rule (this book's cup-of-tea rule)

Book8's hook was an object in the reader's hand (tea, the 38th parallel). This
book's hook is sharper and built-in: **the reader holds the passport these
documents gatekeep, and never sat the exam.** Every chapter: at least ONE moment
where the reader faces the test themselves — a real question they can try (and
plausibly fail), an oath line they never swore, a clause that applies to naturalized
citizens but not to them (FR's déchéance under *Fraternité*), a value they'd have to
affirm 5/5 (AU). It must be a real item from the corpus, never invented. The fixed
coda operationalizes it: "Now take your own country's test."

Landers beyond the test itself (one per chapter max, book8 rules apply): a holiday
whose test-answer origin the reader doesn't know; a citizenship ceremony the reader
has seen in the news; the price of the guide (£12.99 for the UK's self-portrait);
a museum the reader has visited (the Reina Sofía holding the only Guernica question).

## Vocabulary

- **Hinge** — a non-obvious connection the COMPARISON discovered (the atonement
  ladder; narrate-vs-test anti-correlation; the DE typographic seam). Found in
  dossiers, never imported.
- **Lander** — the concrete bridge to ordinary life (a test question the reader
  fails; the passport in their drawer). Analyst win + reader win, both required.
- **Distractor** — a wrong answer written by the state. Treat distractors as quotes:
  cite them exactly, with the question number. A distractor is the state imagining
  error — what it fears people believe, or wants them to stop believing.

## Pitch before writing (the guinea-pig gate — unchanged)

Threads are never drafted cold. Per chapter: 2–4 candidate threads pitched in ≤4
sentences each (seed quote/silence → loop → shared hook); the user is the test
reader; strongest genuine reaction gets written; no reaction = no thread. Pitches
live in drafts/NN-slug.pitches.md.

## Pre-ship test (all five or don't ship)

1. Would a tired adult who hated civics read past the ladder?
2. Does the reader face the test at least once — question, oath, clause, price?
3. Is every enrichment claim checkable in two minutes, marked `<!-- CHECK: ... -->`?
4. Quotes: grep-verified against corpus-orig/ (newline-normalized); FR/UK eye-checked
   per CONTEXT.md provenance caveats; distractors cited with question numbers.
5. **The dinner test**: can the reader retell the chapter in one sentence without
   saying "the guides differ"? ("America's citizenship test accepts 'states' rights'
   as a cause of the Civil War" passes. "Countries frame slavery differently" fails.)

## Two chapter modes (inherited)

- **Exhibit** — short ladder + tell + receipts; thread optional. The zero chapters
  and the machinery chapters live here.
- **Essay** — thread first or central; ladder trimmed to the rows that earn it.
Series identity = kicker + credit + receipts + coda, not all nine beats every time.
One formal mutant per Part, earned. A chapter that is literally a test the reader
takes (questions first, sources revealed after) is the sanctioned mutant candidate
for Part III.

## Who owns the personal (hard rule, inherited)

Reader-generic by default. Author-personal material enters ONLY when the human author
pastes their own life in; the model must not invent or extrapolate it. No "you feel X."
The author's own naturalization-adjacent experience, if any, enters only by their hand.

## Credit where due (calibration)

Every guide gets its installment: FR's slavery box is the most explicit atrocity
acknowledgment in the corpus (1.2M, Nantes, crime against humanity, loi Taubira);
EE names its own SS division; AT names 700,000 NSDAP members; DK prints its own
discrimination statistics and a convicted politician's slur verbatim to mark the
legal line; CA apologizes with a date and an agent. The book's fairness engine:
the same state is often the frankest narrator on one page and the most evasive on
the next — show both or show neither.

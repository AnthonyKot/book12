How this book is made

The corpus

Fifteen positions. Ten full columns — the official citizenship-test study materials of the UK, Canada, Australia, the United States, Germany, Austria, Denmark, France, Spain and Estonia, in their pinned editions. Three special voices that are never treated as peer narrators: Russia (which publishes no narrative — only demonstration exams: an 18-page demo for the temporary-residence track and, since August 2025, a 16-page citizenship demo with its specification), and the Netherlands and Latvia (which publish exam requirements and practice tools but no book — the refusal is the exhibit). And a zero column: Japan, Israel and Belarus, which publish no test material at all, because naturalization there is ministerial discretion. All documents are official state publications, free and public except the UK's, which is Crown copyright and sold.

Corrections made in the open

The Russian document first collected for this book was labelled here as "the citizenship demo". Its own first page says it is the exam for a temporary residence permit (уровень 2). The citizenship-track demo and specification (FIPI/Rosobrnadzor, under Government decree 1136 of 31 July 2025) were collected on 17 August 2026 and are what chapter 6 reads. A book about what states leave out records what it got wrong.

The rules

  • Quotes or nothing. No claim about a document without the passage, original and translation, ending in a page or question number.
  • Absence is data, graded against the baseline. A topic missing from one guide when most carry it is an avoidance signal; a topic missing from nearly all guides is genre norm; a topic only one guide carries is a signature.
  • Wrong answers are quotes. Half this corpus is multiple-choice. The distractors a state writes — the errors it imagines for its applicants — are treated as carefully as the correct answers, and cited by item number.
  • Credit precedes critique. Every document gets its installment: the frankest page of each guide is shown before its most evasive one.
  • The applicant is never the joke. These books are addressed to people passing a harder test than most readers ever took.

The ancestors

This is the sibling of Same event. Different classrooms. (book 8), which read eleven national school history textbooks against each other one event at a time. School textbooks share events; citizenship guides do not — so here the unit of comparison is the slot: the founding story, the worst chapter, the values page, the wrong answers. The ladder — passages side by side, ordered by rhetorical posture, never alphabetically — is inherited, as is the fairness engine and the closing line of every chapter.

Who is talking

The comparison and drafting are done by an AI working under the rules above, with the human author as first reader, test audience, and final editor. Machine translation is used for discovery only; everything quoted is taken from the original page. Two documents carry special handling: the UK guide is quoted from a re-typeset copy and verified against the printed original before shipping, and the French Livret is a scanned document quoted by transcription from the page images.

# Book 12 — Chapter template (nine beats, slot-adapted)

Chapter unit = the **slot**: a question every state's guide must answer (or visibly
decline to answer) — the founding story, the worst chapter, the values page. The
ladder runs across countries within the slot.

## The nine beats

1. **Kicker** — Slot name · Chapter N · which documents sit the exam this chapter.
2. **Lede** — one concrete image, no thesis. Sensory, dated, unarguable.
   Ex.: "In a Sydney exam room, a screen asks five questions that must all be
   answered correctly. None of them mention a date before 1788."
3. **The Question** — ONE sharp question per chapter, mapped to ONE coding category:
   - agency → "Who took whom?" (empire & slavery)
   - distractors → "What does a state write when it invents a wrong answer?"
   - testability → "Why does the state that narrates most test least?"
   - vocabulary → "When is a person 'employed'?" (UK indentured-labour verb)
   - omission → "Where did the applicant's own story go?" (immigration: 0 hits)
4. **The boring facts** — 5–8 lines of undisputed scaffolding: what the exam is,
   pass mark, who publishes, what the law requires. Deliberately dry — the ruler.
5. **The ladder** — side-by-side passages ordered by **rhetorical posture** (e.g.
   atonement grammar: named-and-dated → qualified → agentless → outsourced → absent),
   never alphabetical. Cell = flag · document + edition · original quote + translation
   · one-line tag. Zero-mention badges for the absent ("Mabo: 0 mentions").
6. **The tell** — 2–3 ¶ of grammar-level analysis: passive voice, the load-bearing
   verb, the quantifier ("une part"), the filing decision (which chapter a fact
   lives in is itself a verdict). No outrage — the quotes carry it.
6½. **The thread** — per AGENT.md: pitched first, never drafted cold. The
   entrance-exam rule supplies the hook (a question the reader can fail, an oath
   they never swore).
7. **Credit where due** — which document handled THIS slot best, and what it got
   right. Fairness paid in installments (see AGENT.md calibration list).
8. **Receipts** — document, edition/year, page or question number for every quote;
   distractors cited by item number; "Read the originals" closing block, human-
   readable, with official source URLs (these documents are free and public —
   unlike book8, the reader can download almost the whole corpus tonight).
9. **Coda** — same one line every chapter: **"Now take your own country's test."**

## Worked example — Ch. 3: Empire & slavery

- Lede: a £12.99 paperback sold at every UK post office, chapter 3, "A global power."
- Question (vocabulary/agency): who acts, in the sentence where a million people move?
- Ladder by atonement grammar: CA (named apology, dated) → FR (grey box + Taubira,
  but 1802 in one flat sentence) → DK (numbers, no rebellion, islands "sold") →
  UK (illegal-at-home framing; "employed to replace the freed slaves") →
  US (Q60 agentless "was taken") → ES (esclav*: 0; the 2-year Sephardic fast track
  as the unnamed remedy) → AU (colonis*: 0; settlement only).
- The tell: the verbs — apologized / reconnaît / solgt / employed / was taken / [silence].
- Credit: FR's grey box — the only state that names a crime against humanity in a
  document it hands to applicants.
- Receipts: page/question numbers per CORPUS.md editions.

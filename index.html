The Entrance Exam

Before a state hands you its passport, it hands you a little book about itself and grades you on believing it. Denmark's runs to 243 pages of history; Germany's is 460 questions with no answers published; Russia's is 18 pages; Japan's does not exist. This book reads the official citizenship-test study materials of fifteen states against each other — the shortest authorized self-portraits governments write, and the only history books whose readers sit an exam on them. Every claim ends in an edition and a page or question number.

Part I · The machinery · Chapter 1

Meet the documents

The Austrian booklet opens with two ministers' letters and a welcome from an expert council: "Willkommen und Gratulation!" The Danish textbook, 243 pages, contains no second-person pronoun addressed to its reader; the three occurrences of du and dig are all inside the titles of songs and books. Between those two is the whole range of ways a state can speak to the person it is about to grade. This chapter is one card per document — what it calls itself, how it describes its own exam, and the sentence in which it first looks at you.

The question · address and purpose

Who does the document think it is talking to — and what does it put on page 1?

1 What happened

  • Ten core documents; three special voices (Russia's demos; the Netherlands and Latvia, which publish no book of their own); a zero column (Japan, Israel, Belarus). Editions and files are listed in the Method.
  • Six of the ten carry a purpose statement addressed to the reader (AT, AU, CA, UK, EE, FR); three are question banks with no place for one (US, DE, ES); one is a 243-page narrative that has none.
  • Three documents describe their own exam in detail (AT: eight pages; EE: two; UK: three, "as of January 2013"); three do not describe it at all (DE, ES, DK) — and those are the states that publish the whole bank or the whole syllabus.
  • Cards are ordered by the posture of the address, warmest to coldest — congratulation, welcome, instruction, a president's speech, a warning label, the third person, question stems only, silence.

2 The cards

AT · Mein Österreich — Lernunterlage zur Staatsbürgerschaftsprüfung · Bundesministerium für Inneres, © BMI Dezember 2020, Wien März 2022 · 84 pp.

The exam, as the document states it (p. 14): „Die Staatsbürgerschaftsprüfung besteht aus 18 Fragen (sechs Fragen zu jedem Prüfungsgebiet) zu den drei Prüfungsteilen „Geschichte Österreichs", „demokratische Grundordnung" und „Geschichte meines Bundeslandes"." Four options per item, „von denen zumindest eine, aber höchstens drei richtig sind" (of which at least one but at most three are correct); partial credit; a wrong tick scores zero for the item; pass with at least 3 points in each area or 12 of 18; retakes allowed; two to four months' study at „15 bis 30 Minuten" a day (p. 17); level "der vierten Klasse Hauptschule (§ 10a StbG)" (p. 10).

First look at you (pp. 5, 7, 10): „Sehr geehrte zukünftige Staatsbürgerin! Sehr geehrter zukünftiger Staatsbürger! Die österreichische Staatsbürgerschaft ist ein hohes Gut…" · „Zu diesem wichtigen Schritt möchte ich Ihnen sehr herzlich gratulieren." · „Willkommen und Gratulation! Wir freuen uns, dass Sie sich für die österreichische Staatsbürgerschaft interessieren."

Contents: Einleitung des Expertenrates · Die Broschüre als Lernunterlage · Die Geschichte Österreichs (nine chapters, Frühe Siedler → Aufbruch nach Europa) · Die demokratische Grundordnung (eight) · Lösungsteil (27 Beispielfragen with key, pp. 76–83).

Signature line (p. 10): „Welche das sind, ist nicht unumstritten. Die Subjektivität der Auswahl ist zwangsläufig." — "Which ones those are is not undisputed. The subjectivity of the selection is unavoidable." The only document in the corpus that tells the applicant its history chapter is a choice, and names who made it (p. 11: texts by Rainer Münz and Christian Stadler).

AU · Australian Citizenship: Our Common Bond — testable section · Department of Home Affairs, © Commonwealth of Australia 2020 · 50 pp.

The exam (p. 5): "The citizenship test is a computer-based, multiple choice test in English. It consists of 20 randomly selected questions; and as of 15 November 2020, it will also include five questions on Australian values. To pass the test, you must answer all five of the values questions correctly, with a mark of at least 75 per cent overall." (p. 2:) "All of the information you need to sit the Australian citizenship test is in this book. You are not required to purchase or obtain other citizenship packages…"

First look at you (p. 4, "A message to you"): "Congratulations on choosing to become an Australian citizen. It takes courage, endeavour and commitment to live in a new country and participate fully as a citizen." — "It is the step that will enable you to say 'I am Australian'."

Contents: A message to you · Part 1 Australia and its people · Part 2 Australia's democratic beliefs, rights and liberties · Part 3 Government and the law · Part 4 Australian values · Glossary of testable section. No items printed.

Signature line (imprint page): "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander readers are warned that this publication contains images and/or content relating to deceased persons." — the warning that survives the trim (ch. 5). The pledge (p. 6): "From this time forward, under God,* I pledge my loyalty to Australia and its people… * A person may choose whether or not to use the words 'under God'."

CA · Discover Canada — The Rights and Responsibilities of Citizenship · Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada; 2012 text, 2021 reprint (Ci1-11/2021E-PDF) · 68 pp.

The exam (p. 6): "The citizenship test is usually a written test, but it could be an interview." "Adult applicants 55 years of age and over do not need to write the citizenship test." No count, pass mark or time stated. (p. 2:) "The only official study guide… available from Citizenship and Immigration Canada at no cost. If you use any other material… you do so at your own risk."

First look at you (p. 2): "Welcome! It took courage to move to a new country. Your decision to apply for citizenship is another big step. You are becoming part of a great tradition that was built by generations of pioneers before you."

Contents: Applying for Citizenship · Rights and Responsibilities · Who We Are · Canada's History · Modern Canada · How Canadians Govern Themselves · Federal Elections · The Justice System · Canadian Symbols · Canada's Economy · Canada's Regions · Study Questions (p. 52, no key: "All the answers can be found in this study guide") · Memorable Quotes.

Signature line (p. 2, "Understanding the Oath"): "In Canada, we profess our loyalty to a person who represents all Canadians and not to a document such as a constitution, a banner such as a flag, or a geopolitical entity such as a country." — printed under the 2021 oath, whose one clause about a text is about the Constitution ("which recognizes and affirms the Aboriginal and treaty rights of First Nations, Inuit and Métis peoples"). Our file carries both oaths: the 2021 text on pdf p. 2, the 2012 text on p. 3, colophon "Ci1-11/2012E" still present.

UK · Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents · Home Office / TSO, 3rd ed. 2013 · ~180 pp. (98 in our third-party re-typeset; eye-check against the TSO original before ship) · sold, £12.99 (ch. 3).

The exam (p. 7): "The test consists of 24 questions about important aspects of life in the UK. Questions are based on ALL parts of the handbook." "Usually taken in English, although special arrangements can be made… in Welsh or Scottish Gaelic"; "about 60 test centres"; (p. 5:) "There are currently (as of January 2013) two ways you can be tested on these requirements"; "There is a fee for submitting an application" (amount not stated).

First look at you (p. 4): "Britain is a fantastic place to live: a modern, thriving society with a long and illustrious history." … "Passing the Life in the UK test is part of demonstrating that you are ready to become a permanent migrant to the UK."

Contents: 1 The values and principles of the UK · 2 What is the UK · 3 A long and illustrious history · 4 A modern, thriving society · 5 The UK Government, the law and your role. No items printed; official practice questions sold separately.

Signature line (p. 6): "There is no place in British society for extremism or intolerance." Runner-up, the pledge: "I will give my loyalty to the United Kingdom and respect its rights and freedoms…"

EE · Abiks Eesti kodakondsuse taotlejale / What you need to know if you are applying for Estonian citizenship · Integration and Migration Foundation Our People, Tallinn 2010 (EN 2011), "Second edition, revised and updated", "Published for free distribution" · 70/67 pp.

The exam (EN pp. 63–64): "Taking the examination is free of charge." "The examination is in written form and is composed of 24 multiple-choice questions." "Forty-five minutes is provided… You pass the examination if you answer at least 18 out of the 24 questions correctly." Dictionaries provided in the room — "Estonian-English" in the English edition, „eesti-vene sõnaraamat" (Estonian-Russian) in the Estonian one. A free consultation before the exam.

First look at you (p. 4, President Ilves at a citizenship ceremony, 30 September 2008): "Your will shows that you trust and need the state of Estonia. I assure you that the state of Estonia also trusts and needs you. The state of Estonia recognises your will." The Estonian text says the state supports „iga siin elava mittekodaniku tahet" — every non-citizen living here; the English says "every stateless person living here."

Contents: Introduction · An overview of Estonian history · Estonia today · The state and the people · Estonia and international organisations · Organisation of governance · Rights, freedoms and responsibilities · Citizenship of the Republic · Examination on the Constitution and Citizenship Act · Sample questions (24, keyed; history: 0).

Signature line (p. 4): "Estonia is my country! My country that I make better, fairer and more beautiful with my work, my care, my commitment and, above all, my love."

FR · Livret du citoyen · Direction générale des étrangers en France, Ministère de l'Intérieur, "Edition mai 2026" (page footer; published July 2026) · 83 pp. · page images only — transcriptions pending eye-check

The exam (p. 3): « Pour constituer votre dossier, vous participerez à un examen civique pour évaluer vos connaissances. Si votre dossier est recevable, vous serez prochainement convié(e) à un entretien en préfecture, en sous-préfecture ou en consulat, afin d'évaluer votre degré d'assimilation à la communauté nationale. » Language level B2. The syllabus of the examen civique is the arrêté of 10 October 2025 (p. 81); no items in the booklet.

First look at you (p. 3): « Vous avez souhaité déposer une demande d'acquisition de la nationalité française, et vous manifestez ainsi votre volonté de rejoindre la communauté nationale, de respecter ses valeurs et de l'enrichir. »

Contents (p. 4): 1 Principes et valeurs de la République · 2 Système institutionnel et politique · 3 Droits et devoirs des citoyens · 4 Histoire, géographie et culture · 5 Vivre dans la société française · Annexes (Déclaration de 1789 · Charte des droits et devoirs du citoyen français · Ressources).

Signature line (p. 3): « Ce livret, sans être exhaustif, illustre le niveau de connaissances attendues de tout(e) candidat(e) à la naturalisation. »

US · Civics (History and Government) Questions for the Naturalization Test · USCIS, 2008 version, rev. 01/19 · 11 pp.

The exam (p. 1): "The civics test is an oral test and the USCIS Officer will ask the applicant up to 10 of the 100 civics questions. An applicant must answer 6 out of 10 questions correctly to pass the civics portion of the naturalization test." Twenty asterisked items for applicants aged 65+ with twenty years' residence. Some answers are live: Q28, Q29, Q47 "Visit uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates for the name of the President…"

First look at you (p. 1): "As you study for the test, make sure that you know the most current answers to these questions." Otherwise the reader is "the applicant", third person; there is no welcome and no purpose statement.

Contents: American Government (Principles · System · Rights and Responsibilities) · American History (Colonial Period and Independence · 1800s · Recent) · Integrated Civics (Geography · Symbols · Holidays). The answers are the document.

Signature line (p. 1): "Although USCIS is aware that there may be additional correct answers to the 100 civics questions, applicants are encouraged to respond to the civics questions using the answers provided below." — and: "The USCIS Officer will not accept an incorrect answer."

DE · Gesamtfragenkatalog zum Test „Leben in Deutschland" und zum „Einbürgerungstest" · Bundesamt für Migration und Flüchtlinge, Stand 07.05.2025 · 191 pp.

The exam: not described anywhere in the catalogue. Structure: Teil I Allgemeine Fragen, Aufgaben 1–300; Teil II, ten items for each of the sixteen Länder. No answer key published.

First look at you: none. Page 1 carries only a notice: „Hinweis: Die Formulierungen von einzelnen Fragen und Antworten können leicht von den verwendeten Originalfragen abweichen." There is no address; „Sie" appears inside question stems and options — Aufgabe 274: „Sie haben in Deutschland absichtlich einen Brief geöffnet, der an eine andere Person adressiert ist. Was haben Sie nicht beachtet?"

The seam: ten of the 300 general items use a different bullet glyph (a hollow □) from the other 290 — Aufgaben 59, 66, 96, 111, 118, 149, 182, 184, 206, 288 — and every one concerns Jewish life, antisemitism, the Holocaust or Israel; two repeat their own number inside the stem. These are the items reported as added in 2024. Aufgabe 111: „Welche Handlungen mit Bezug auf den Staat Israel sind in Deutschland verboten? □ die Politik Israels öffentlich kritisieren □ das Aufhängen einer israelischen Flagge auf dem Privatgrundstück □ eine Diskussion über die Politik Israels □ der öffentliche Aufruf zur Vernichtung Israels". The revision is visible in the typography.

Signature line (p. 1): the Hinweis above — the state warns that its own published questions may differ from the ones you will get.

ES · Manual de preparación de la prueba de conocimientos constitucionales y socioculturales de España (CCSE) 2026 · Instituto Cervantes, © 2025 · 102 pp.

The exam: not stated in the manual. The manual is the bank: 300 items numbered by task (1001–1120, 2001–2036, 3001–3024, 4001–4036, 5001–5084), three options each, key on pp. 99–101; a quarter renewed each year.

First look at you: none. Usted: 0 hits in 102 pages. The reader surfaces inside two stems in the first and second person — item 1005 «¿Cuándo puedo hacer los trámites en la sede electrónica?» and item 4026 «¿En qué museo español puedes ver el cuadro Guernica de Picasso?»

Contents: Tarea 1 Gobierno, legislación y participación ciudadana · 2 Derechos y deberes fundamentales · 3 Organización territorial. Geografía física y política · 4 Cultura e historia de España · 5 Sociedad española · Soluciones.

Signature line (p. 57): «ACONTECIMIENTOS RELEVANTES EN LA HISTORIA DE ESPAÑA (1252-2019) — Algunos de los acontecimientos de la historia de España más conocidos internacionalmente desde el s. XIII hasta la actualidad son:» — history as a table of what foreigners already know.

DK · Læremateriale til Indfødsretsprøven — danske samfundsforhold, dansk kultur og historie · Udlændinge- og Integrationsministeriet / SIRI, august 2025 ("Redaktionen er afsluttet juni 2025") · 243 pp.

The exam: not described. Apart from the cover, the colophon and the running header, the word indfødsretsprøve never occurs in the body. The applicant's own exam appears once, in the third person, as a fact of Danish political history (p. 57, on the 2001 tightening): «Endvidere må man ikke begå alvorlig kriminalitet, og man skal bestå en række prøver i det danske sprog samt i dansk historie, samfund, kultur og værdier.» — "one must pass a series of tests in the Danish language and in Danish history, society, culture and values."

First look at you: none. No foreword, no message, no second person. Du/dig: 3 hits, all inside titles («Hva' gør vi nu, lille du?»; «Har døden taget noget fra dig…»; «Prix du Jury»).

Contents: Kap. 1 Danmarks historie (thirteen sections, Vikingetid → 1990–) · 2 Det danske demokrati · 3 Den danske økonomi · 4 Danmark og omverdenen · 5 Dansk kulturliv · 6 Temaopslag (seventeen, from geography to «Diskrimination, antisemitisme, hadforbrydelser og ekstremisme»). No items.

Signature line (p. 5): «Den danske stat har eksisteret som selvstændig stat uden afbrydelser i over 1.000 år, og det er ret enestående set i et internationalt perspektiv.» Runner-up (p. 6): «Det er ikke en fuldstændig og udtømmende Danmarkshistorie.»

Special voices

RU · two demonstration exams, no narrative. (a) Демонстрационный вариант, уровень 2 (2023): p. 1 header — «…на уровне, соответствующем цели получения разрешения на временное проживание в Российской Федерации (уровень 2)» — the temporary-residence track; 18 pp., 34 items (21 language, 7 history, 6 law), key p. 14; © Rosobrnadzor. (b) FIPI, «Демонстрационный вариант КИМ для проведения экзамена по истории России и основам законодательства РФ для целей приобретения гражданства РФ» (2025): 16 pp., 36 items (18 history, 18 law; 3 open-answer with marking criteria), maximum 40 points; a 13-page specification with a codifier of mandated content (1.1 «862 г.» → 1.26 «Начало Специальной военной операции…»); legal basis Presidential decree 889 of 22.11.2023 and Government decree 1136 of 31.07.2025. First look at you: «Вы» capitalised in the instructions; «участник экзамена» in the rubric; no purpose statement. Signature item: citizenship demo Q22 — the applicant is asked under which three conditions "Алекс" may apply for citizenship, and one correct condition is that «Алекс подтвердил в установленном законом порядке знание истории России и основ законодательства Российской Федерации»: the exam tests you on the rule that you must pass the exam.

NL · no book. The state fixes the examinable learning outcomes in law (Eindtermen KNM, Staatscourant 2024, operative from 1 July 2025) and provides browser practice exams through DUO; instruction is left to commercial publishers. The split between what the state defines and what it declines to write is the exhibit (ch. 11). LV · no free state guide. A PMLP self-test tool ("18 questions: 10 history/culture and 8 Constitution") and a study book the state sells; the official four-page brochure was unreachable when collected. JP · IL · BY · nothing. Ministerial discretion (JP, IL); no test material located (BY); no published test corpus, no self-portrait — verified against the ministries' pages in the corpus manifests.

3 The tell

Line the first-look sentences up and read only their verbs. Gratulieren. "Congratulations." "Welcome." "…is part of demonstrating that you are ready." "The state of Estonia recognises your will." « Vous manifestez ainsi votre volonté. » "The USCIS Officer will ask the applicant." Then nothing, nothing, nothing. The order is not random: the three documents with the warmest greetings (Austria, Australia, Canada) print no answer key or no items; the three that publish the most items (Germany, Spain, the United States) never speak to the reader except inside a question; and the longest document in the corpus, Denmark's, is also the coldest — 243 pages of syllabus in which the applicant's exam is mentioned once, as something "one" must pass, in a paragraph about a 2001 change in the law. The state that writes most looks at you least. Genre explains the banks' silence; it does not explain Denmark's, and Spain's manual wraps its bank in prose and still finds no room for usted.

Where the exam is described is a filing decision too. Austria spends eight pages on it, with a study calendar and the sentence "Raten zahlt sich nicht aus" — guessing does not pay. Estonia spends two, with the words "free of charge." Britain spends three, "as of January 2013," in a book still on sale. Germany, Spain and Denmark spend none: the format lives on a website, and the document is the whole bank or the whole syllabus. Three states say the same self-sufficiency sentence — "You are not required to purchase… other citizenship packages" (AU), "you do so at your own risk" (CA), "Everything that you will need to know… is included in this handbook" (UK) — and one of the three says it about a book it sells.

Editions leak, and the leaks are data. Canada's file carries two oaths, the 2021 text with the Aboriginal-and-treaty-rights clause on one page and the 2012 text without it on the next, under a 2012 gloss that loyalty is "not to a document such as a constitution." Spain's 2026 manual is © 2025. Austria's is © December 2020, printed March 2022. Britain's says "From October 2013, the requirements will change" in a book sold in 2026. Germany's catalogue is dated 7 May 2025 and the ten items added in 2024 still sit in a different bullet glyph. Estonia's English edition swaps the exam-room dictionary from Estonian-Russian to Estonian-English and turns "every non-citizen living here" into "every stateless person" — the translation changed who the reader was.

Sit this · the reader's items

AT, Beispielfrage 2 (key p. 76): „Die Staatsgebiete welcher heutigen Staaten gehörten im Laufe der Geschichte zeitweise zum Kaiserreich Österreich? Slowakei / Kroatien / Ungarn / Finnland" — three of four are correct; tick one and you lose the item. US, Q54: "How old do citizens have to be to vote for President?" — and then Q67, the Federalist Papers. EE, sample Q23 ("Which of the following sentences is true?"): "Estonian citizenship may be granted to people for the services they have rendered to the state, but not to more than 10 such people per year." UK, the pledge (p. 6): "I will give my loyalty to the United Kingdom and respect its rights and freedoms. I will uphold its democratic values. I will observe its laws faithfully and fulfil my duties and obligations as a British citizen." — read it aloud once; the British-born are never asked to.

4 Credit where due

Austria's booklet handles the machinery best: it states the count, the scoring, the partial credit, the pass rule, the retake, the level of difficulty and a study calendar; it names the authors of its history; and it admits, in print, that the selection is subjective and "erhebt aber keinen Anspruch auf Vollständigkeit" — makes no claim to completeness. No other document tells the applicant how it was made. Estonia is the runner-up: "free of charge," 45 minutes, 18 of 24, a dictionary in the room, a free consultation — the most complete practical page in the corpus, in a book the state still links fifteen years on. The credit stands before the Limits note what each of the two leaves for the website.

5 Where this stops being true

Limits

Three question banks and two demos have no place for a welcome; their silence toward the reader is a format effect, and the finding that survives it is narrower: a 243-page narrative that says nothing, and a bank wrapped in prose that says nothing. Purpose statements are carried by six of ten — absence in the banks is genre, absence in Denmark a signature. Facts about the exams that the documents do not state — fees, pass marks, time limits, attempts — are not asserted here; the one verified figure is Britain's £50 test fee (ch. 3), and the rest are left to the states' websites. Canada's file duplicates each spread in extraction and carries two oath layers, so nothing in Canada is counted by hits; Britain's re-typeset scrambles the order of chapter 3; France is transcribed from three page images (pp. 3, 4, 81) and nothing else about it is claimed. Russia's cards are demos, not the approved bank, and the 2023 file's own header — the residence-permit track — is a correction this book made to its own corpus. The Netherlands, Latvia and the zero column have no document to card and are described from the collection manifests only.

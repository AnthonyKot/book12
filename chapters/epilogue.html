Epilogue · The reader sits the paper

Could you pass?

Eleven chapters of reading over the shoulders of people taking an exam, and one question left. Below are eight real items, drawn one per document from the states that print items at all. They are copied as their governments print them, in the language the applicant sits them in, with a translation for reading and nothing else — no country named, no key, no hint of which chapter of this book they came from. Answer them first. The sources, the keys and the pass marks are on the other side of the ruled line. Four more states are represented after that, by the only thing they publish: the rule.

Part one · the paper

Eight items. Nothing is invented; every one is printed in a document a state hands to people applying for its citizenship. Two of them have no published answer, and that is not an omission in this book.

1 The paper

1. "What is one responsibility that is only for United States citizens?"

2. „Wie viele Einwohner hat Deutschland? ▪ 70 Millionen ▪ 78 Millionen ▪ 84 Millionen ▪ 90 Millionen“
"How many inhabitants does Germany have? ▪ 70 million ▪ 78 million ▪ 84 million ▪ 90 million"

3. „Ab wann dürfen österreichische Staatsbürger/innen wählen? ▪ Frauen ab 18 Jahren ▪ Männer ab 18 Jahren ▪ Männer und Frauen ab 16 Jahren ▪ Das ist abhängig vom jeweiligen Bundesland“
"From what age may Austrian citizens vote? ▪ women from 18 ▪ men from 18 ▪ men and women from 16 ▪ it depends on the province"

4. «¿Quién puede presentar una queja al Defensor del Pueblo? a. Solo los ciudadanos legalmente residentes. b. Solo los españoles mayores de 18 años. c. Todos los ciudadanos, españoles o extranjeros.»
"Who can bring a complaint to the Ombudsman? a. Only legally resident citizens. b. Only Spaniards over 18. c. All citizens, Spanish or foreign."

5. "Which of the following sentences is true? 'A minister may be a member of parliament at the same time.' ▪ True ▪ False"

6. "What did the Canadian Pacific Railway symbolize?"

7. «Супруги решили расторгнуть брак. При каком условии в Российской Федерации этот вопрос может быть решён только в суде? 1) Супруги прожили вместе более трёх лет. 2) У супругов есть общие несовершеннолетние дети. 3) Супруги ведут совместный бизнес.»
"A married couple has decided to dissolve their marriage. Under what condition can this matter, in the Russian Federation, be decided only by a court? 1) The spouses have lived together more than three years. 2) The spouses have children in common who are minors. 3) The spouses run a business together."

8. "jānorunā vai jāuzraksta valsts himna"
"the national anthem must be recited or written out." Not a question — an instruction. Write out your own national anthem now, from memory, on paper. That part is graded separately from the rest.

Before the line: two of those eight have no published key anywhere. One of them is a multiple-choice item in a catalogue of 460 questions whose state publishes no answers at all; the other is an open question in a booklet that says only that the answer "can be found in this study guide." If you found yourself certain about either, that certainty came from somewhere other than the document.

2 The reveal

Item Where it comes from, and what the state accepts
1 · US
Q49, asterisked
Civics Questions for the Naturalization Test, 2008 version (rev. 01/19), p. 5. Two answers are printed and both are accepted: "▪ serve on a jury ▪ vote in a federal election." The asterisk means it is also on the short list of items the state offers applicants aged 65 or over with twenty years' residence. The next question in the bank asks for a right that is only for citizens, and answers it with voting and running for federal office. Pass mark, printed on p. 1 of the same document: "An applicant must answer 6 out of 10 questions correctly." The officer chooses the ten, orally, and "will not accept an incorrect answer."
2 · DE
Aufgabe 213 — no key exists
Gesamtfragenkatalog zum Test „Leben in Deutschland“ und zum „Einbürgerungstest“, BAMF, Stand 07.05.2025, p. 79. The catalogue prints 460 items and not one answer, and warns on page 1 that the wording "may differ slightly from the original questions used." So this book will not tell you either — that is the exhibit. What can be said is that the four options are twenty million apart at the ends and six million apart in the middle, and that the state has published nothing that settles it. Pass mark: not stated in the document.
3 · AT
Beispielfrage 15
Mein Österreich, BMI, 2022, p. 58; the key is in the Lösungsteil on p. 80, and marks the third option: „Männer und Frauen ab 16 Jahren“. The booklet's own prose says it plainly two chapters earlier: „Wählen dürfen österreichische Staatsbürger/innen ab 16 Jahren.“ Sixteen is the answer here and a printed wrong answer in the German catalogue's federal item on voting age. Pass mark, p. 16: at least 3 points in each of the three exam areas, or at least 12 points in total out of 18 — and a wrong tick scores nothing for the whole item, because "Raten zahlt sich nicht aus."
4 · ES
CCSE bank, Tarea 1
Manual CCSE 2026, Instituto Cervantes (© 2025), p. 24; the key on p. 99 gives c: all citizens, Spanish or foreign. The manual prints all three hundred items and their answers and never states how many questions the exam asks, how long it lasts, or how many must be right. This item's number is detached from its text in the two-column extraction and is 1084 by inference from the key page — eye-check before ship. Pass mark: not stated in the manual.
5 · EE
Sample question 16
What you need to know about the Republic of Estonia (EN 2011), p. 66. The key is the asterisk, and it is on False: in Estonia a minister may not sit in the Riigikogu at the same time. Any reader from a Westminster system — Britain, Canada, Australia — has just failed an item their own country's arrangements would have told them to get wrong. Pass mark, p. 64: "You pass the examination if you answer at least 18 out of the 24 questions correctly." Forty-five minutes, free of charge, in Estonian, with the Constitution, the Citizenship Act and a dictionary on the desk — and "a new examination is produced every time an examination is taken."
6 · CA
Study question — no key printed
Discover Canada, 2012 text (2021 reprint), study questions on folio 52: "The questions below are similar to the questions that are found on the citizenship test … All the answers can be found in this study guide." They are not printed with the questions. The answer is on folio 20, in a sentence about the last spike: "On November 7, 1885, a powerful symbol of unity was completed when Donald Smith (Lord Strathcona), the Scottish-born director of the Canadian Pacific Railway (CPR), drove the last spike." Unity — and the sentences that follow record that the line was "built by both European and Chinese labour" and that "Afterwards the Chinese were subject to discrimination." Pass mark: not stated in the guide, which says only that the test "is usually a written test, but it could be an interview."
7 · RU
FIPI citizenship demo, Q35 — special voice
Демонстрационный вариант, экзамен по истории России и основам законодательства для целей приобретения гражданства, FIPI, 2025, p. 11; the key on p. 13 is 2: children in common who are minors. This is family law, and it is the mildest item on the paper. Elsewhere the same demo grades the applicant's wording for Crimea and has them list the grounds on which a citizenship "obtained not by right of birth" can be terminated. Pass mark, Спецификация p. 7: not fewer than 28 primary points of 40 for the whole paper, of which at least 12 in Russian history and at least 12 in the foundations of legislation; not more than 90 minutes (p. 6). Russia publishes the most complete set of exam rules in this corpus and no narrative at all.
8 · LV
The instruction, not an item
PMLP leaflet Latvijas pilsonība, 4 pp., file dated 4 June 2021, fetched 2026-08-17. The knowledge test is the anthem — recited or written — plus questions on Latvian history and culture and on the basic provisions of the Constitution, and "Katra daļa tiek vērtēta atsevišķi": each part is graded separately. The leaflet prints the fee, "Pamatnodeva 28,46 eiro", the reduced fee, the towns, the exemptions, and who decides — the Cabinet of Ministers — and not one date from Latvian history. That is in a book the same office sells. The state publishes a sample paper and a browser self-test; neither is in this corpus (see ch. 11).

3 The four states whose questions cannot be printed

  • Britain — 24 questions, "based on ALL parts of the handbook," and "The 24 questions will be different for each person taking the test at that test session." No item exists in the handbook to print; official practice questions are sold separately. The pass mark is not in the book. re-typeset — TSO eye-check pending
  • Australia — "It consists of 20 randomly selected questions; and as of 15 November 2020, it will also include five questions on Australian values. To pass the test, you must answer all five of the values questions correctly, with a mark of at least 75 per cent overall." Not one of the twenty-five is printed. The state adds: "You are not required to purchase or obtain other citizenship packages from any individuals or organisations."
  • Denmark — 243 pages, no item, and no sentence anywhere in the book about how many questions there are or how many must be right. The exam's name appears on the cover, in the colophon and in the running header, and nowhere in the prose.
  • France — a civic examination and an interview at the prefecture "afin d'évaluer votre degré d'assimilation à la communauté nationale," with the syllabus in Annexe I of a ministerial order of 10 October 2025. The booklet has no text layer in this corpus and no items in it. provisional — page-image transcription
  • The Netherlands — the exam's content is fixed as law in the government gazette and the state's own page says "Het examen duurt 45 minuten." There is no state book to sit; the instruction is a commercial product. Japan and Israel publish statutory conditions and a minister's discretion. Belarus: nothing was found, and the page the search rested on returned nothing when it was checked again on 17 August 2026.

4 How you did, and what it means

Six of the eight items above have a published answer; two do not, and both of those belong to states that examine a great deal. If you got five or six of the six, you would still have failed the German test on a technicality — because there is no German answer to compare yourself against, and there are 460 items to guess at rather than eight. If you missed the Estonian one, that is the shape of the whole book in miniature: knowing how your own country works is not knowledge of how another one does, and every applicant who passes one of these papers has had to unlearn something true about home.

Five of these states print the number you need in the document itself: the United States, Austria, Australia, Estonia and Russia. Britain, Canada, Germany, Spain, Denmark and France do not — the reader can find those numbers, but not in the book their applicants are given. Which means that for six of the eleven documents in this corpus, the applicant is told to study everything and never told what counts as enough.

And one last asymmetry, the one that runs under all eleven chapters. Every reader of this book holds a citizenship that was never examined. Nobody asked you the population of your country, or which of your ministers may sit in your parliament, or what your railway symbolised, or the words of your anthem — and nobody can take that citizenship back on the strength of a wrong answer. In Russia the applicant is examined on the list of crimes for which a citizenship "obtained not by right of birth" is terminated. In Estonia the same page that tells the applicant to renounce their old passport records that a citizenship obtained by birth "cannot be taken away from anyone." That sentence is the honest one. It is also, in eight of the eleven documents, the sentence nobody thought to print.

So: your own country. Find its study guide, if it has one, and check four things — whether the question bank is published, whether the answers are, whether the pass mark is printed in the book rather than on a website, and who signs the decision at the end. Then find one item in it you would get wrong.
